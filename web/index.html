Where the edge is

Top setups

put BWB, 1 lot = +1 upper / −2 short / +1 lower · $ per lot · click a column to rank, a row for its risk graph

Priced from a live TWS chain (frozen closing quotes outside market hours): own IV from mids off the put-call-parity forward, one smile per expiry, every leg priced from it. The exit scenario holds each strike's IV (sticky strike) and lets time pass; the odds use a lognormal with the chosen volatility, which understates crash risk, so check the −1σ and Stress columns and the risk graph too. Stress = index −5% by the exit date with every strike's IV 5 points higher, a typical sell-off. Exit date = your average days held for that entry DTE (Journal) unless you type one. "Your usual" = upper long within ±1% of spot and short 0.5–3.5% below, like your A14 entries in ONE. Risk = the loss below the lower wing (or the debit, if larger).

Cloud snapshot

your trade views on the GitHub copy, encrypted on this laptop with your passphrase

What goes up: Risk grid with the last TWS marks, Trade plan, Capital use, Journal, Evidence, P&L & Recon, account summary, the last recorded calendar / skew / expected-move / A14 scans (default settings) and today's pick. Account numbers, e-mail addresses and local paths are replaced before encryption; the passphrase never leaves this laptop and is not stored (only a key derived from it, in data/). The ciphertext is pushed to the repo's snapshot branch automatically whenever something it shows changes (at most every 20 minutes); the cloud page asks for the passphrase once per browser session. Use at least 12 characters, ideally four or more unrelated words: the ciphertext is public, so the passphrase is the only protection. Setting a new passphrase re-encrypts on the next publish and locks every browser that remembered the old one.

IBKR Flex statements

official daily NAV, deposits and commissions, read once a day

The token and Query ID stay on this laptop in data/ and go only to IBKR; the desk can't show them again, so saving new ones replaces them. A statement can only be read: it can't trade. Each day's statement comes out the next morning, and Update all collects it. Its NAV replaces the desk's own capture of that day, and its deposits and transfers come off the P&L. A token lasts at most a year: when it expires this panel turns red and Data check flags it.

Trade readiness — the size call

        
    
          
  1. One question, one answer: how big should new premium-selling trades be today? Full, three-quarter, half, quarter, or stand aside.
    2. 
          
  2. Every input speaks the same language: how often SPX fell 5% or more within the next 21 sessions after days like today (normally about 17%). Inputs: the VIX level, the active VIX conditions, the five Market warnings with history since 2010, and sector rotation (offense vs defense).
    3. 
          
  3. The verdict uses the highest of those odds (they overlap, so adding them would double count): under 17% full, 17–23% three-quarter, 23–30% half, 30%+ or a sit-out condition (HYG −1.5% day, VIX 40+) stand aside.
    4. 
          
  4. Each structure can then step down for your own rules and the 2009–2026 backtest: A14 when the index is below its 20-day average, VIX/VIX3M is 0.90+ (flat: one step; backwardation: stand aside) or RUT premium is thin; calendars stand aside at VIX/VIX3M 0.90+ and step down below the 20-day average or into CPI / FOMC / payrolls / PCE / GDP.
    5. 
          
  5. One VIX/VIX3M scale everywhere: ≤ 0.834 steep contango (calm; the best calendar zone since 2009), 0.834–0.90 normal, 0.90–1.00 flat (stress building; A14 size down, calendars avoid), ≥ 1 backwardation. Two trends everywhere: short-term = vs the 20-day average, long-term = vs the 200-day.
    6. 
          
  6. It reflects history, not a forecast: rougher odds have also come with higher average returns (selling after drops), so treat it as position sizing, not direction.
    7. 
        

      

      
Today's pick

        
    
          
  1. VIX conditions (top): the VIX inputs on their own (their chance of a 5% drop; the size call is the readiness bar), the live readings (VIX, VIX − realised, VVIX, VIX9D/VIX, RVX − VIX, SPX vs its 200-day, HYG), and each active condition with what followed it historically (next 21 days, chance of a 5% drop vs normal). Sit-out conditions: HYG −1.5% day, VIX 40+. Cautions: backwardation, negative premium, SPX below 200-day, VIX jumping into highs, VVIX high with calm VIX, complacent sell-offs, VIX9D above VIX, thin RUT premium. Opportunities: backwardation just ended, rich premium, rich RUT premium, HV7, VIX up 5 days running. Open "All conditions" for the full history table. The Market tab's Overview shows which conditions are on, with a link here.
    2. 
          
  2. It compares four choices: put calendar (time spread) or A14 put BWB (single expiry), on SPX or RUT.
    3. 
          
  3. Each gets the best setup from the same TWS chain, held for your Exit after days (a calendar at most to the day before its front expiry), with the same odds.
    4. 
          
  4. Edge / day = expected P&L at exit as % of risk, per day held — so structures held for different times compare fairly.
    5. 
          
  5. Your own rules are checked: calendars need VIX/VIX3M below 0.90, the index above its 20-day average and the TimeEdge / Triple / TimeZone gates; A14 wants the index above its 20-day average. ✓ / ✕ shows each.
    6. 
          
  6. The pick = the best edge among setups passing all your rules. If none pass, it says so — size down or wait.
    7. 
          
  7. Update all refreshes the chain, and it also runs by itself after the close. Open Next best for alternatives.
    8. 
        

      

      
Market tab

        
    
          
  1. Regime / Market warnings — same rules as your Market Warnings v2 Pine script. Count of 6; 3 or more is a warning cluster. The odds behind the size call use the five with history since 2010.
    2.

    3.

    4. 
          
  4. Sectors — three plain-English reads: risk appetite (offense vs defense sectors — falling often comes before volatility picks up), how broad leadership is, and whether RUT-heavy sectors are beating SPY (helps choose SPX vs RUT). The chart tracks offense ÷ defense; bars rank each sector against SPY.
    5. 
          
  5. Calendar — US economic events; toggle Medium / High / Both.
    6. 
          
  6. Accounts — balances and today's fills for every sub-account.
    7. 
          
  7. Auto-refresh is set at the top (default every 10 minutes); Refresh forces it.

Term structure, Calendar scanner, Skew & IV

        
    
          
  1. Term structure — today's SPX vol curve and the TimeEdge / Triple Calendar / TimeZone entry rules with ✓/✕ for each check.
    2. 
          
  2. Calendar scanner — pick SPX or RUT and how to rank (contango, contango z-score, forward premium, forward vol − realised). Press Scan.
    3. 
          
  3. Read the heatmap: rows = front expiry, columns = back expiry, green = better. Click a cell to see that pair's history. In the Top pairs table, hover the # badge for why a pair ranks there. Click a row: below its contango history is the calendar's risk graph with your 15% / 20% stops and the overnight move that hits each. The strategy gates also check VIX/VIX3M below 0.90, the index above its 20-day average and no CPI / FOMC / payrolls / PCE / GDP within 3 days, the same rules as the readiness bar.
    4. 
          
  4. Rule of thumb from the 2009–2026 backtest: calendars below VIX/VIX3M 0.90 with the index above its 20-day average; avoid 0.90+ and downtrends. (The old "avoid ≤ 0.834" rule came from your June 2–4 2026 losses, which the backtest reproduces; over 17 years that band was otherwise the best zone.)
    5. 
          
  5. Releases column — CPI, FOMC, payrolls, PCE and GDP between today and the front expiry; amber when it includes CPI, FOMC or payrolls. FOMC dates come from the Fed, the rest from TradingView about 30 days out, so "+?" or "unknown" means the front runs past what is known. Click a row for IV at each strike: front, back, spread and forward vol vs RV20. Your calendars shows the same for open calendars at their own strikes. All of this is information: gates, ranking, Today's pick and the size call don't use it.
    6. 
          
  6. Skew & IV — ATM IV, put skew and their z-scores. |z| ≥ 2 = unusual. z-scores fill in as daily recordings accumulate (20 sessions).

TWS has none of these for past days. Official NAV is exact over weeks, but a single day can swing and reverse on IBKR's closing marks, so today's change still compares the desk's own captures.
    6. 
          
  6. Cloud snapshot (Recorder tab) — set a passphrase once and the laptop pushes an encrypted copy of the trade views (risk grid with the last marks, trade plan, journal, evidence, P&L, last scans) to the GitHub copy whenever they change.     7. 
          
  7. Trade plan — every open trade with card rules gets one action, checked in this order: hard stop → target → time stop → slow (under half your pace target after 3+ weeks, or 25% past your usual hold) → decide line → hold. A14: target +5% (entered at 21 DTE or less) or +7%, decide line −6%, hard stop −10%, out at 2 DTE; Allantis the same with +7%. Calendars follow the card (TimeEdge +5% / −15% / 1 DTE, TimeZone +5% / −5% / 7 DTE) with one adjustment, then exit. Adjust? applies gates from your own adjustment history: never above +3%; only when red or the underlying is ±1.5% from entry; reshape only above 21 DTE (10–21: roll out or close). The detail panel shows the levels in dollars and the ±1.5% band. Adjustment counts come from the newest ONE Detail Report in Documents or Downloads. The table's Plan column carries the same action.
    8.

Trades under half the target from here, or so far after 3+ weeks, are listed as slow with the margin they tie up and the daily gap to target; past usual hold marks trades held 25% longer than your closed A14s with the same entry DTE. The trade table has a Pace now column with fast / slow tags. The Journal's Overview shows the same pace by month closed.
    9. 
          
  9. Whole book chart — every trade added together against a % market move.
    10.

Below it, per account: SPX-weighted delta, the worst cell, and the excess liquidity left after it. That figure is a best case, because the margin requirement also rises in a real move.
    11. 
          
  11. Health line — on each trade card, in the detail panel and in Do today: total against stop and target, days in trade against your usual hold, short strikes in σ, and whether an adjustment is allowed. Short strikes turn amber only when spot is 0.5σ or more through one, since around the short is a BWB's best zone.

Expected moves

        
    
          
  1. Update all refreshes these; Refresh from TWS does only this tab (about a minute). It also runs by itself after the close.
    2. 
          
  2. Each underlying you hold gets a card: the cone chart (±1σ dark band, ±2σ light band) with your strikes as dots (red = short, green = long).
    3. 
          
  3. The table lists each expiry: ATM IV, straddle price, the 1σ move and the 1σ / 2σ price ranges. held marks expiries you are in.
    4.

A14 scanner (put BWBs on SPX and RUT)

        

          
    
            
  1. Fresh chain: Update all refreshes it, and it also runs by itself after the close. Outside market hours it uses Friday's / yesterday's closing quotes.
    2. 
            
  2. Check the trend panel first. Your own A14s entered above the 20-day average made more than those entered below it, but both made money. In the 2009–2026 model, below the average with VIX/VIX3M between 0.834 and 0.90 was the best A14 cell, so the readiness bar doesn't step down for it there; in steep contango (rare) or a flat term structure it still does. See the size rules in the ribbon's Why pop-up.
    3. 
            
  3. Set the filters: Underlying, DTE window (default 14–45, where your A14s earned the most per day held), Exit after (leave blank to use your typical hold for each entry DTE, from your closed A14s), Odds from (realised 20-day vol is the default), Min credit (−1 allows up to a 1-point debit), Upper long range in % from spot. Press Scan.
    4. 
            
  4. Read the heatmap ("Where the edge is"): rows = expiries, columns = short strike % below spot, greener = better expected return. Click a cell to filter the table to it.
    5. 
            
  5. Pick from the table. Rank by E/risk per day (default; rows tagged fast meet your pace target, slow earn under half of it), E/risk (capital use — divides by your real holding period, so it compares short and long expiries fairly), Stress loss (smallest loss in a −5%, IV +5 sell-off first), θ/risk, P(profit) or profit/risk. Tick your usual shapes only to match how you normally build them. Hover the # badge for why a setup ranks there: what it is ranked on, its strengths (For) and what to check (Watch).
    6. 
            
  6. Click a row for its risk graph: today, after your exit days, and at expiry, with the ±1σ zone shaded. Check the −1σ at exit and Stress at exit losses before placing it (red dashed line = the exit curve with IV 5 points higher). A setup marked fragile loses more than half its risk in the stress.
    7. 
            
  7. Build it in ONE / TWS yourself — the desk never sends orders.
    8. 
          

          

            
Reading a row

            
    
              
  • Strikes 2820 / 2790 / 2740 = +1 upper put, −2 short puts, +1 lower put (per lot).
    • 
              
  • Wings 30/50 = upper wing 30 points, lower wing 50 points.
    • 
              
  • Credit + = you receive money (no loss if the market rallies); − = debit.
    • 
              
  • Risk = the loss if the market finishes below the lower wing.
    • 
              
  • E/risk = expected P&L at your exit date as % of risk. P(profit) = chance of being in profit then.
    • 
              
  • Profit zone at exit = the spot range (from today) where you are in profit on your exit date.
    • 
              
  • Short σ = how far below spot the short strike is, in standard deviations to expiry.
    • 
              
  • your usual = like your past A14 entries. trend ↑ / below 20d = the underlying's trend now.
    • 
            

            
The odds assume normal-shaped moves, which understate crash risk. Treat the ranking as a shortlist, not a signal. "Your A14 history" at the bottom shows how your own past entries did by DTE.

  1. Evidence — every closed calendar-family trade against the market conditions on its entry day. Use it to test a rule before trusting it.
    2. 
          
  2. Evidence → Since 2009 — your A14 shape and the TimeEdge calendar replayed on every entry day since late 2009 with the model that matched your ONE fills, after costs: exit rules by DTE, results by VIX/VIX3M band and trend, the worst entries, and re-centring by rule. This is where the calendar gate (VIX/VIX3M below 0.90, index above its 20-day average) and the A14 −20% stop come from. Recomputed after each new close.

Glossary

        
    
          
  • DTE — days to expiry. DIT — days in trade.
    • 
          
  • σ (sigma) — one standard deviation of the expected move; spot × IV × √(days/365).
    • 
          
  • IV / RV — implied volatility (priced by options) / realised volatility (what the index actually did, 20 days).
    • 
          
  • VIX/VIX3M — 30-day vs 3-month vol. Below 1 = contango (normal); at or above 1 = backwardation (stress).
    • 
          
  • Contango (calendars) — back-month IV minus front-month IV. Forward vol — the vol implied between two expiries.
    • 
          
  • BWB — broken-wing butterfly: lower wing wider than the upper. A14 — your put-BWB strategy on SPX/RUT.
    • 
          
  • Delta per 1% — $ change for a 1% market move. Theta — $ per day from time decay. Vega — $ per 1 vol point.
    • 
          
  • NAV — net liquidation value of the account (AUD, the base currency).
    • 
          
  • z-score — how unusual today is vs recent history; beyond ±2 is rare.
    • 
          
  • Frozen quotes — closing prices TWS returns when the market is shut.

What you seeWhat to do

"No marks" / "TWS not reachable"Start TWS and log in, wait 90 seconds, then press Update all. Check TWS → API settings allow connections on port 7496.

          
Red "ONE export is …h old" bannerExport a fresh ONE Summary Report. Differences may just be trades not yet in ONE.

          
Risk grid trades out of dateExport a fresh ONE Summary Report to Documents, then reload the page.

          
Recon shows quantity differencesUsually a fill not yet entered in ONE, or an expired/assigned leg. Use the ONE import CSV or fix the trade in ONE.

          
A week's P&L looks too bigCheck for a deposit. Connect IBKR Flex in the Recorder tab, or add it under risk.cash_flows in config.json.

          
A14 scanner / Expected moves look stalePress Update all. The time of the data is shown next to each tab's button.

          
Market charts show "no data"Press Refresh on the Market tab. Breadth builds in the background after start-up (a few minutes).
