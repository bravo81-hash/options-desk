Cloud snapshot

your trade views on the GitHub copy, encrypted on this laptop with your passphrase

What goes up: Risk grid with the last TWS marks, Trade plan, Capital use, Journal, Evidence, P&L & Recon, account summary, the last recorded calendar / skew / expected-move / A14 scans (default settings) and today's pick. Account numbers, e-mail addresses and local paths are replaced before encryption; the passphrase never leaves this laptop and is not stored (only a key derived from it, in data/). The ciphertext is pushed to the repo's snapshot branch automatically whenever something it shows changes (at most every 20 minutes); the cloud page asks for the passphrase once per browser session. Use at least 12 characters, ideally four or more unrelated words: the ciphertext is public, so the passphrase is the only protection. Setting a new passphrase re-encrypts on the next publish and locks every browser that remembered the old one.

IBKR Flex statements

The token and Query ID stay on this laptop in data/ and go only to IBKR; the desk can't show them again, so saving new ones replaces them. A statement can only be read: it can't trade. A token lasts at most a year: when it expires this panel turns red and Data check flags it.

  1. It starts on its own from login, at http://127.0.0.1:8810. Opening TradingView or TWS opens the page.
    2. 
          
  2. TWS must be running for live data. Without it the desk shows the last saved data and falls back to free sources.
    3. 
          
  3. Update all refreshes every source in turn. It also runs by itself every 15 minutes while the US market is open and hourly otherwise. The Data pill shows each source's date and last result.
    4. 
          
  4. ONE: export the Detail Report each day to Documents. A Summary Report still works for the book, P&L & Recon and the Evidence tab, whichever is newest, but the Journal's entry risk, peak risk and adjustments come from the fills only the Detail Report has.
    5. 
          
  5. Finding your way: the sidebar badges show what's live and ⓘ notes open on a click. The sun and moon switch sets light or dark mode.
    6. 
          
  6. Cloud copy: market views are public. Your trades stay locked until you press the snapshot chip at the top and enter your passphrase. They are as of the snapshot, and Update all, TWS refreshes and the Recorder are laptop only.
    7. 
        

      


      
Daily routine (10 minutes)

        
    
          
  1. Now: the desk opens here, and in the trading hour it is the only screen you need. It answers three things (can I open anything, what do I do with what I hold, am I within my limits), asks for one tap on how the next week will go, and shows the automatic log of whether each decision followed the rules. Show detail in the side bar brings back every other tab; nothing is removed.
    2. 
          
  2. Rules: every rule the desk applies, in one list, each with what it is, why it is there and who set it. The numbers come from the code that enforces them, so the page is always current.
    3. 
          
  3. Ribbon: read the size for each structure, and the Event risk chip beside it (green, amber or red, with the stress count and any alerts). If the Data pill is amber, something is older than the last close.
    4. 
          
  4. Actions: work down Do today, New trades and Coming up. Under them, the Event risk, Overnight, Stress, Pre-close, Market conditions, Orders & alerts and Data check tabs open on a click, and a second click closes them. Data check opens by itself when something is out of date; Pre-close opens by itself from 14:30 to 16:00 ET.
    5. 
          
  5. P&L & Recon: Reconciliation should say Quantities match for both accounts. If not, fix ONE before trusting the Risk grid.
    6. 
          
  6. Risk grid: check P&L now, Delta per 1% and Worst in range. Expected moves shows where your short strikes sit from spot.
    7. 
          
  7. Opening something new? Start at Today's pick, then use the Calendar or A14 scanner for alternatives.
    8. 
          
  8. After the US close the desk records by itself: surface at 15:40 ET, then expected moves and the A14 chain, final NAV at 16:15 ET.
    9. 
        

      


      
Trade readiness — the size call

        
    
          
  1. One question, one answer: how big should new premium-selling trades be today? Full, three-quarter, half, quarter, or stand aside.
    2. 
          
  2. Every input speaks the same language: how often SPX fell 5% or more within the next 21 sessions after days like today (normally about 17%). Inputs: the VIX level, the active VIX conditions, the five Market warnings with history since 2010, and sector rotation (offense vs defense).
    3. 
          
  3. The verdict uses the highest of those odds (they overlap, so adding them would double count): under 17% full, 17–23% three-quarter, 23–30% half, 30%+ or a sit-out condition (HYG −1.5% day, VIX 40+) stand aside.
    4. 
          
  4. Each structure can then step down for your own rules and the 2009–2026 backtest: A14 when the index is below its 20-day average, VIX/VIX3M is 0.90+ (flat: one step; backwardation: stand aside) or RUT premium is thin; calendars stand aside at VIX/VIX3M 0.90+ and step down below the 20-day average or into CPI / FOMC / payrolls / PCE / GDP.
    5. 
          
  5. One VIX/VIX3M scale everywhere: ≤ 0.834 steep contango (calm; the best calendar zone since 2009), 0.834–0.90 normal, 0.90–1.00 flat (stress building; A14 size down, calendars avoid), ≥ 1 backwardation. Two trends everywhere: short-term = vs the 20-day average, long-term = vs the 200-day.
    6. 
          
  6. It reflects history, not a forecast: rougher odds have also come with higher average returns (selling after drops), so treat it as position sizing, not direction.
    7. 
        

      

      
Event risk, overnight, stress

        
    
          
  1. Information only. None of this changes the size call, the gates, the rankings or Today's pick. It's there because a calm-looking market at the close can still gap on what reports that night.
    2. 
          
  2. Event risk: red when over 15% of the S&P 500 reports within 24 trading hours, or payrolls, CPI, FOMC or an election falls inside them. Amber at 5% or more, a top-10 name reporting, another high-impact release, or a top-tier release or over 15% inside 48 trading hours. Weekends and market holidays don't count. With a feed down and nothing else to flag it reads Unknown, never green.
    3. 
          
  3. Overnight: ES, NQ and RTY against their own price at the cash close (alert over 0.75%), VIX futures front vs second month at the last settlement, and the top 10 names out of hours (alert over 5%). Leader spillover: a top-10 name or SMH down 3σ or 7% in a day flags next-day risk; other leaders are listed without raising an alert. A break warns about the next session, so its alert ends at that session's close; the table keeps the row, marked expired.
    4. 
          
  4. Stress: how many of 11 signals are firing across the vol curve, breadth and cross-assets, with the last 30 sessions. It's a separate count from the Market warnings (of 6). Amber at 3, red at 5.
    5. 
          
  5. Pre-close: event risk, net gamma, the ±2σ overnight P&L and, when event risk is red, a reference price for a put spread. It opens by itself from 14:30 to 16:00 ET.
    6. 
        

      

      
Today's pick

        
    
          
  1. VIX conditions (top): the VIX inputs on their own (their chance of a 5% drop; the size call is the readiness bar), the live readings (VIX, VIX − realised, VVIX, VIX9D/VIX, RVX − VIX, SPX vs its 200-day, HYG), and each active condition with what followed it historically (next 21 days, chance of a 5% drop vs normal). Sit-out conditions: HYG −1.5% day, VIX 40+. Cautions: backwardation, negative premium, SPX below 200-day, VIX jumping into highs, VVIX high with calm VIX, complacent sell-offs, VIX9D above VIX, thin RUT premium. Opportunities: backwardation just ended, rich premium, rich RUT premium, HV7, VIX up 5 days running. Open "All conditions" for the full history table. The Market tab's Overview shows which conditions are on, with a link here.
    2. 
          
  2. It compares four choices: put calendar (time spread) or A14 put BWB (single expiry), on SPX or RUT.
    3. 
          
  3. Each gets the best setup from the same TWS chain, held for your Exit after days (a calendar at most to the day before its front expiry), with the same odds.
    4. 
          
  4. Edge / day = expected P&L at exit as % of risk, per day held — so structures held for different times compare fairly.
    5. 
          
  5. Your own rules are checked: calendars need VIX/VIX3M below 0.90, the index above its 20-day average and the TimeEdge / Triple / TimeZone gates; A14 wants the index above its 20-day average. ✓ / ✕ shows each.
    6. 
          
  6. The pick = the best edge among setups passing all your rules. If none pass, it says so — size down or wait.
    7. 
          
  7. Update all refreshes the chain, and it also runs by itself after the close. Open Next best for alternatives.
    8. 
        

      

      
Market tab

        
    
          
  1. Regime / Market warnings — same rules as your Market Warnings v2 Pine script. Count of 6; 3 or more is a warning cluster. The odds behind the size call use the five with history since 2010.
    2. 
          
  2. Breadth — % of S&P 500 and Nasdaq-100 stocks above their 20/50/200-day averages. Below 20 = washed out, above 80 = stretched. "Narrow" = index up on few stocks.
    3. 
          
  3. Volatility — VIX with bands, the term structure (9D→1Y), implied vs realised (option sellers are paid when implied is above realised), and VIX/VIX3M for the year. The blue line at 0.834 is the edge of steep contango, the amber line at 0.90 the calendar gate.
    4. 
          
  4. Sectors — three plain-English reads: risk appetite (offense vs defense sectors — falling often comes before volatility picks up), how broad leadership is, and whether RUT-heavy sectors are beating SPY (helps choose SPX vs RUT). The chart tracks offense ÷ defense; bars rank each sector against SPY.
    5. 
          
  5. Calendar — US economic events; toggle Medium / High / Both.
    6. 
          
  6. Accounts — balances and today's fills for every sub-account.
    7. 
          
  7. Auto-refresh is set at the top (default every 10 minutes); Refresh forces it.
    8. 
        

      


      
Term structure, Calendar scanner, Skew & IV

        
    
          
  1. Term structure — today's SPX vol curve and the TimeEdge / Triple Calendar / TimeZone entry rules with ✓/✕ for each check.
    2. 
          
  2. Calendar scanner — pick SPX or RUT and how to rank (contango, contango z-score, forward premium, forward vol − realised). Press Scan.
    3. 
          
  3. Read the heatmap: rows = front expiry, columns = back expiry, green = better. Click a cell to see that pair's history. In the Top pairs table, hover the # badge for why a pair ranks there. Click a row: below its contango history is the calendar's risk graph with your 15% / 20% stops and the overnight move that hits each. The strategy gates also check VIX/VIX3M below 0.90, the index above its 20-day average and no CPI / FOMC / payrolls / PCE / GDP within 3 days, the same rules as the readiness bar.

FOMC dates come from the Fed, the rest from TradingView about 30 days out, so "+?" or "unknown" means the front runs past what is known. All of this is information: gates, ranking, Today's pick and the size call don't use it.

Trade plan — every open trade with card rules gets one action, checked in this order: hard stop → target → time stop → slow (under half your pace target after 3+ weeks, or 25% past your usual hold) → decide line → hold. A14 from the rule page: target +7%, decide line −6%, hard stop −20%, out by 7 DTE, whatever the entry DTE; Allantis +7%, −6%, −10%, 2 DTE. A trade is labelled only from a total with every leg priced: when a leg has no live quote it uses the session's last fully priced total and says as of when, and with neither it shows Marks incomplete (only the time stop still applies), which never goes into the Journal's plan log. Calendars follow the card (TimeEdge +5% / −15% / 1 DTE, TimeZone +5% / −5% / 7 DTE) with one adjustment, then exit. Adjust? applies gates from your own adjustment history: never above +3%; only when red or the underlying is ±1.5% from entry; reshape only above 21 DTE (10–21: roll out or close). The detail panel shows the levels in dollars and the ±1.5% band. Adjustment counts come from the newest ONE Detail Report in Documents or Downloads. The table's Plan column carries the same action.
    8. 
          
  8. The trade table has a Pace now column with fast / slow tags. The Journal's Overview shows the same pace by month closed.
    9. 
          
  9. Whole book chart — every trade added together against a % market move.
    10. 
          
  10. Gap what-if — the book at the next open for moves of 1.5%, 3%, 5%, 7% and 10% each way, and at where futures are now, in total and per account. Columns: IVs moving with the index (+1.5 points per 1% fall, −1 per 1% rise), then the VIX down 3, flat, up 3 or up 6. A switch picks whether the recorded smile slides with the index (the default, and the stress check's basis: on the recorded days since 9 Oct it came closest on falls and was the harsher of the two on rises) or each strike keeps its IV (2–3 times the actual IV rise on recorded falls). RUT moves 1.2 × the SPX move on the ± rows; the Futures row uses RTY's own move. How often counts the SPX closes that moved at least that much in a session since 1990, and each account shows its worst cell as % of NAV.
    11. 
          
  11. What-if — the whole book at ±1σ and ±2σ with IV down 3, unchanged, or up 5 or 10 points, at a horizon you pick (σ comes from the VIX). Below it, per account: SPX-weighted delta, the worst cell in US$ and % of NAV, and the excess liquidity left after it (a best case, because the margin requirement also rises in a real move). Then how often SPX has really fallen 3%, 4% and 5% in a day against what the VIX's σ suggests, and Book limits: A14 ids per ticker against the cap of 4, the largest id's worst case against the rule's 7.5% of NAV, and the book on a 5% day either way, IVs moving with it, as % of NAV against your stress limit once you set one (config.local.json, risk.stress.limit_pct). Breaches show at the top of Actions. Information only.

Health line — on each trade card, in the detail panel and in Do today: total against stop and target, days in trade against your usual hold, short strikes in σ, and whether an adjustment is allowed. Short strikes turn amber only when spot is 0.5σ or more through one, since around the short is a BWB's best zone.

  3. Use it for timing and sizing: a weak or high-vol seasonal window is a reason to size down short-put risk or pick shorter expiries; compare its realised vol with today's implied vol on Expected moves.
    4. 
          
  4. Months: average return, % of years up, realised vol and VIX change by month, plus every year's monthly returns. Days & cycle: turn-of-month, weekday and presidential-cycle stats.
    5. 
        

      

      
Expected moves

        
    
          
  1. Update all refreshes these; Refresh from TWS does only this tab (about a minute). It also runs by itself after the close.
    2. 
          
  2. Each underlying you hold gets a card: the cone chart (±1σ dark band, ±2σ light band) with your strikes as dots (red = short, green = long).
    3. 
          
  3. The table lists each expiry: ATM IV, straddle price, the 1σ move and the 1σ / 2σ price ranges. held marks expiries you are in.
    4. 
          
  4. Your strikes (σ from spot): e.g. −16 7625P −0.9σ = you are short 16 of the 7625 put, 0.9 standard deviations below spot for that expiry.
    5. 
          
  5. How to use it: about 68% of outcomes fall inside ±1σ by expiry. A short strike inside −0.5σ is close to the action; outside −1.5σ is far. The 1-day move at the top is the typical daily swing.

The A14 rules
SPX and RUT, from 2 Nov 2026

        
    
          
  • Size: 1.5% of NAV lost at the −20% stop, so 7.5% of NAV worst case an id. Only the readiness bar scales it.
    • 
          
  • Lots: The same lots in every account, worked out on the smallest NAV, so none goes over 1.5%.
    • 
          
  • Exit: +7% target, −20% stop, out by 7 DTE. Watched every day.
    • 
          
  • Adds: None. New risk is always a new id.
    • 
          
  • Ladder: At most 4 ids per ticker and account. Risk grid → What-if → Book limits checks the open book against the cap and the 7.5%, and Actions shows any breach at the top.
    • 
          
  • Entry: One id per ticker a week, the Friday expiry nearest 49 DTE (42–56): on the Friday, or earlier in the week when the readiness bar reads full or three-quarter size for that ticker and every account is inside its limits (from 12 Nov 2026). A midweek id replaces that week's Friday entry. Skip it with 4 open.
    • 
          
  • Regime: One structure. The tent sits 0.2 sd higher with the index more than 1% above its 20-day average, 0.2 sd lower more than 1% below, centred otherwise.
    • 
          
  • Strikes: The desk's sd shape, shown in Actions → New trades. No hand-picking.
    • 
          
  • Adjust: Only with the id's delta beyond ±3 a lot (from 12 Nov 2026; before that, on Monday, Wednesday or Friday), never on a trade up 3% or more, and none at 21 DTE or less.
    • 
          
  • Scoreboard, not a target: since 10 Nov 2026 the desk scores drawdown from each account's peak, rules followed and your reads of the week ahead (P&L → Scoreboard). The 1%-a-week target is retired. Your own limits sit above the rule page: all A14 worst cases under your cap and a 5% day under your stress limit, per account; over either, the ladder shows no new id.
    • 
        

        
Ids entered before 2 Nov keep their own lots and strikes and aren't scored against this page, though the Trade plan applies the 7 DTE exit and the delta band to them from now on. The Journal shows how older trades would have scored only with hindsight switched on. Flags, not orders.

      

      
What the numbers said
model, every Friday since 2009

        
    
          
  • Exit at 7 DTE: beat 2 DTE at every entry DTE and halved the losses over 50% of margin.
    • 
          
  • Entry at 42–56 DTE: beat entries at 35 DTE or less. A −5% and +10 vol shock costs about $3,100 per $10k of worst case at 45 DTE, against $6,000 at 14 and $8,500 at 7.
    • 
          
  • Cap of 4: cost almost nothing against no cap, with less swing.
    • 
          
  • Daily stops: checking stops only three days a week raised losses over 50% of margin from 0.6% to 1.8% of trades, so targets and stops are watched every day.
    • 
          
  • Vega and gamma: vega is about 2–3% of worst case at every DTE, so capping worst case caps vega. A 1% index move costs about a day's theta; the 7 DTE exit keeps gamma out of the last week.
    • 
          
  • One structure: three regime versions added under a point a trade. Regime pays through size, which is the readiness bar's job.
    • 
        

        
Read this with the target. The long-run model earns far less than recent live months did, which had no big market shock: about +0.02% of NAV a week per 1% of NAV of worst case an id, positive in 13 of 18 years. At this size that's about +0.15% a week, with a worst week near −10% and a worst drawdown near −53%. Four ids per ticker on two tickers is about 60% of NAV worst case. A 5-day SPX fall of 5% or more has come about 2.5 times a year since 2009.

      

      
A14 scanner (put BWBs on SPX and RUT)

        

          
    
            
  1. Fresh chain: Update all refreshes it, and it also runs by itself after the close. Outside market hours it uses Friday's / yesterday's closing quotes.
    2. 
            
  2. Check the trend panel first. Your own A14s entered above the 20-day average made more than those entered below it, but both made money. In the 2009–2026 model, below the average with VIX/VIX3M between 0.834 and 0.90 was among the best A14 cells, so the readiness bar doesn't step down for it there; in steep contango (rare) or a flat term structure it still does. See the size rules in the ribbon's Why pop-up.
    3. 
            
  3. Set the filters: Underlying, DTE window (default 28–70, two weeks either side of the rule's 42–56 entry window; rows inside it are tagged entry window), Exit after (leave blank to use your typical hold for each entry DTE, from your closed A14s), Odds from (realised 20-day vol is the default), Min credit (−1 allows up to a 1-point debit), Upper long range in % from spot. Press Scan.
    4. 
            
  4. Read the heatmap ("Where the edge is"): rows = expiries, columns = short strike % below spot, greener = better expected return. Click a cell to filter the table to it.
    5. 
            
  5. Pick from the table. Rank by E/risk per day (default; rows tagged fast meet your pace target, slow earn under half of it), E/risk (capital use — divides by your real holding period, so it compares short and long expiries fairly), Stress loss (smallest loss in a −5%, IV +5 sell-off first), θ/risk, P(profit) or profit/risk. Tick your usual shapes only to match how you normally build them. Hover the # badge for why a setup ranks there: what it is ranked on, its strengths (For) and what to check (Watch).
    6. 
            
  6. Click a row for its risk graph: today, after your exit days, and at expiry, with the ±1σ zone shaded. Check the −1σ at exit and Stress at exit losses before placing it (red dashed line = the exit curve with IV 5 points higher). A setup marked fragile loses more than half its risk in the stress.
    7. 
            
  7. Build it in ONE / TWS yourself — the desk never sends orders.
    8. 
          

          

            
Reading a row

            
    
              
  • Strikes 2820 / 2790 / 2740 = +1 upper put, −2 short puts, +1 lower put (per lot).
    • 
              
  • Wings 30/50 = upper wing 30 points, lower wing 50 points.
    • 
              
  • Credit + = you receive money (no loss if the market rallies); − = debit.
    • 
              
  • Risk = the loss if the market finishes below the lower wing.
    • 
              
  • E/risk = expected P&L at your exit date as % of risk. P(profit) = chance of being in profit then.
    • 
              
  • Profit zone at exit = the spot range (from today) where you are in profit on your exit date.
    • 
              
  • Short σ = how far below spot the short strike is, in standard deviations to expiry.
    • 
              
  • your usual = like your past A14 entries. trend ↑ / below 20d = the underlying's trend now.
    • 
            

            
The odds assume normal-shaped moves, which understate crash risk. Treat the ranking as a shortlist, not a signal. "Your A14 history" at the bottom shows how your own past entries did by DTE.

Positions and R

        
    
          
  1. Position: all of a trade's fills from the ONE Detail Report, adjustments included. A losing trade reopened in the same ticker within the hour counts as one position (a roll).
    2. 
          
  2. Decision: the same trade placed in MGN and SMSF is one decision, so one win or one loss. R averages the two and dollars add up. Twins named differently take the strategy they share, in the Journal only: Evidence and the gates keep the report's own names.
    3. 
          
  3. 1R is the position's worst case at entry: what it loses if every leg ends at intrinsic value, at any price. It stays frozen. Risk added later shows as Peak ÷ 1R, not as a bigger 1R. R = P&L ÷ 1R.
    4. 
          
  4. Planned loss is the card's stop × 1R. The cap (warning 1%, cap 2% of NAV, in USD at the day's AUD/USD close) is measured on this. Trades with no card stop have no planned loss and stay out of it.
    5. 
          
  5. Return on risk-days: P&L ÷ (risk held × days), each span at the risk it carried then, so a trade that grew isn't flattered.
    6. 
          
  6. Information only. Nothing in the Journal changes sizing, gates or orders. The cloud copy reads it all; typing happens on the laptop.
    7. 
        

      

      
The Journal's sub-tabs

        
    
          
  1. Trades: folders by strategy on the left, one trade in full on the right, with an editable note (Use the suggestion restores the generated one) and tags for how the trade felt. Table: every position, sortable; click a closed trade for its card.
    2. 
          
  2. Overview: cumulative P&L, days held vs return, totals by strategy and month, capital pace.
    3. 
          
  3. Edge: the numbers, the cumulative R curve with its drawdowns shaded, and the R histogram. Mean under median means a few big losses pull the mean. Skew stays grey: short premium reads negative by nature.
    4. 
          
  4. Confidence: each strategy against the rules of thumb: losses against the 30 bar (ONE's plus OptionStrat's before June), an 80% Wilson range on win rate, an 80% bootstrap range on mean R, CV and skew. A losing strategy never passes CV. There is no overall verdict, and ⚑ marks under 20.
    5. 
          
  5. Discipline: rule accuracy against win rate. A rule counts only from the day it began: cards 24 Oct, entry gates 25 Oct, cap 1 Nov. The box shows every trade scored in hindsight. Trade plan's Exit and Close calls and the Adjust? answer are logged when Actions opens, then held against the fills that follow. Overrides go here.
    6. 
          
  6. Risk taken: planned loss against the cap, risk added after entry, open risk by account, and New pre-commit: write down what you accept as gone before the trade. Each is stamped written before or after the fill.
    7. 
          
  7. Behaviour: results and size after winning streaks and after losses, revenge flags, mood against result.
    8. 
          
  8. Review: losses past 2.5× the average, or past their planned loss, owe a post-mortem. The count shows on the Journal badge and in Actions.
    9. 
          
  9. Settings: every threshold, plus a backup of what you typed. No report can rebuild that, so it also travels in the encrypted snapshot.

Glossary

        
    
          
  • DTE — days to expiry. DIT — days in trade.
    • 
          
  • σ (sigma) — one standard deviation of the expected move; spot × IV × √(days/365).
    • 
          
  • IV / RV — implied volatility (priced by options) / realised volatility (what the index actually did, 20 days).
    • 
          
  • VIX/VIX3M — 30-day vs 3-month vol. Below 1 = contango (normal); at or above 1 = backwardation (stress).
    • 
          
  • Contango (calendars) — back-month IV minus front-month IV. Forward vol — the vol implied between two expiries.
    • 
          
  • BWB — broken-wing butterfly: lower wing wider than the upper. A14 — your put-BWB strategy on SPX/RUT.
    • 
          
  • Delta per 1% — $ change for a 1% market move. Theta — $ per day from time decay. Vega — $ per 1 vol point.
    • 
          
  • NAV — net liquidation value of the account (AUD, the base currency).
    • 
          
  • z-score — how unusual today is vs recent history; beyond ±2 is rare.
    • 
          
  • Frozen quotes — closing prices TWS returns when the market is shut.
    •
